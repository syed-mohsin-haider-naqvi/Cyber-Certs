# Notes — ISC2 Certified in Cybersecurity (CC) Domains

Notes per domain, matching the 5 domain structure ISC2 actually uses. Each domain had its own short course and certificate, so keeping these separated the same way rather then merging them into one big block.

---

## Domain 1 — Security Principles

This is the foundation domain, focuses on the fundamental rules of security. Goal here is keeping company data private, accurate, and available — CIA triad again basically, which at this point ive seen covered in basically every single course ive done (Intro to Cybersecurity, Network Defense, the Udemy course, now here). Keeps reinforcing how central it actually is though, not just repetitive for no reason.

Covers best practices like the NIST framework specifically, used to actually identify risks systematically instead of just guessing, and making sure security efforts tie back to what the business actually needs instead of security existing in its own bubble disconnected from business goals.

---

## Domain 2 — Business Continuity, Disaster Recovery & Incident Response

This domain is about preparing for stuff going wrong before it actually happens. Creating and testing crisis playbooks — having an actual defined plan for what to do when an issue arises instead of figuring it out in the moment during an actual crisis.

Main goal is enabling a fast return to normal operations after a system failure so the business can keep running. Connects pretty directly to the incident response and disaster recovery stuff cisco covered in Cyber Threat Management module 6, good reinforcement of that material from a slightly different angle.

---

## Domain 3 — Access Controls Concepts

Managing data access specifically. Involves implementing strict systems — MFA (multi-factor authentication) being the main one mentioned — to make sure people only have access to what they actually need, nothing more. The overall aim is solid data security through controlled, deliberate access rather then leaving things open by default.

This is basically the AAA concept (authentication, authorization, accounting) from Network Defense module 3, just framed here specifically around the access control angle rather then the broader AAA framework.

---

## Domain 4 — Network Security

Creating a secure pathway for digital information moving across a network. Firewalls and other tools get used here to detect network threats specifically. A strong defense needs to protect data while its actually in transit — whether thats simple office wifi traffic or data moving to/from the cloud specifically.

Overlaps with alot of Network Defense from cisco (firewalls, ZPF, ACLs all live in that territory) but this domain covers it at a much higher, more conceptual level rather then actual configuration — makes sense given ISC2's CC is meant to be entry level and vendor neutral, not cisco-specific hands on config work.

---

## Domain 5 — Security Operations

The continuous, ongoing effort to actually maintain security day to day, not a one time setup. Constant monitoring of systems, including log reviews specifically to identify vulnerabilities before they turn into real incidents. When a problem does come up, a response gets initiated including actually resolving the issue, and security software helps enable a faster reaction time then a fully manual process would.

This domain is the closest one to actual SOC analyst day to day work out of all 5 — monitoring, log review, initiating response — basically describing the job itself at a conceptual level.

---

## Overall

Doing these 5 domains alongside the cisco courses worked well as reinforcement more then totally new material — almost everything here overlapped with something covered in more depth somewhere in the cisco series (Network Defense, Cyber Threat Management especially). What ISC2 adds thats genuinely useful is the standardized vocabulary and domain structure specifically, since this is the same terminology that shows up in job postings and other certs like Security+ — good to have both the cisco depth and the ISC2 standard framing of the same underlying concepts.

Got a completion certificate for each of the 5 domains individually. Havent sat the actual proctored CC exam yet — thats a separate ~$50 step im holding off on for now, the domain certs just show ive gone through the material itself.
